## **The three phases of agent autonomy**

When we think about agent autonomy, we tend to see it as all-or-nothing: either an agent is autonomous or it isn’t. But for incident response, the reality is more like a three-phase progression.

The first phase is invoked: copilots, chat interfaces, and CLIs that wait for an on-call engineer to open them and ask a question mid-incident. The second is background: scheduled jobs, alert rules, and triggers that fire when conditions are predicted. Both phases depend on a human noticing that something is wrong and deciding to act.

The third phase marks an important shift: [proactive agents](https://thenewstack.io/the-future-of-ai-in-sre-preventing-failures-not-fixing-them/) decide for themselves when an incident needs attention, without waiting to be invoked or scheduled.

## **What changes once agents act on their own**

With many teams and tools stuck in one of the first two phases, Dwivedi will discuss where the shift to full autonomy is already happening, drawing on Traversal Workers already operating at the proactive end of the progression. He will also explore [what changes engineering teams](https://thenewstack.io/the-agentic-revolution-a-new-vision-for-sres/) will need when the agent in their stack stops waiting to be asked.
